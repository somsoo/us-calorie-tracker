📖 Clinical Methodology: The Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

Daily metabolic energy expenditure is determined using the validated Mifflin-St Jeor formula, widely endorsed across clinical and athletic dietetics literature (Mifflin et al., American Journal of Clinical Nutrition):

  • Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) + 5
  • Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) multiplies your BMR by your physical activity multiplier (1.2 to 1.9). Calorie targets are adjusted according to your rate of fat loss or muscle gain, while preserving essential hormonal function.

Clinical references: Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics Evidence Analysis Library; Dietary Guidelines for Americans (USDA & HHS); ISSN Position Stand: Diets & Body Composition.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the baseline caloric energy expended by your vital organs at complete physical and digestive rest. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) factors in physical activity and non-exercise thermogenesis to determine total daily caloric maintenance.

How accurate is the Mifflin-St Jeor formula?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is recognized by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics as the most reliable non-laboratory predictive equation for resting metabolic rate in healthy adults, typically predicting within 10% of indirect calorimetry.

How are macronutrient grams calculated?

Protein and carbohydrates provide 4 calories per gram, while dietary fats provide 9 calories per gram. The calculator multiplies your daily caloric target by each nutrient's percentage and divides by the respective caloric density.

Are my dietary entries or body measurements saved online?

No. All metabolic formulas and food log records run exclusively on your browser device. Zero personal biometrics or food records are transferred or stored on remote servers.
